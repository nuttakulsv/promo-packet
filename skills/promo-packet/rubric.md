# A generic IC rubric: senior, staff, principal

Use your company's ladder if it has one. This is a fallback, distilled from public ladders and common practice. Each level includes the one below it.

## 1. Scope and impact

| Senior | Staff | Principal |
|---|---|---|
| Owns a feature or service end to end. Impact is visible to the team and its users. | Owns problems that span several teams or a whole product area. Impact shows up in the org's goals, not only the team's. | Owns problems that span the company, over years. Changes what the company can do. |

**Convincing evidence:** a before and after with numbers (latency, cost, incidents, revenue, time to ship); a problem that was blocking several teams and isn't any more; work that other teams now build on.
**Not convincing:** number of PRs, lines of code, hours, "I was very busy".

## 2. Technical direction

| Senior | Staff | Principal |
|---|---|---|
| Makes sound design choices for the team's systems. Writes designs others can review. | Sets direction for an area: writes the design docs and decisions others follow, picks what not to build, and makes decisions stick across teams. | Shapes technical strategy across the org; anticipates problems a year or more out. |

**Convincing:** an RFC or ADR that was adopted, and what happened because of it; a decision you made that saved a team from a dead end; a migration you planned that others carried out.
**Not convincing:** "I proposed many ideas", without what happened to them.

## 3. Execution on ambiguous work

| Senior | Staff | Principal |
|---|---|---|
| Delivers well-defined projects predictably, breaks them down, flags risk early. | Takes a vague, cross-team problem, turns it into a plan, de-risks the hard part first, and lands it with other teams doing most of the work. | Lands multi-year, multi-org efforts; knows when to stop one. |

**Convincing:** the project that had no owner until you; the risk you found early (a prototype, a load test, a spike) that changed the plan; the thing you stopped.
**Not convincing:** "delivered on time", with no sense of how hard it was.

## 4. Making others better

| Senior | Staff | Principal |
|---|---|---|
| Mentors juniors, gives useful reviews, onboards new people. | Raises the bar beyond their team: reviews in other repos, shared tools and standards, grows senior engineers, improves how the org works. | Grows staff engineers; shapes engineering culture. |

**Convincing:** someone you mentored who got promoted or took over something; a tool or standard other teams adopted; reviews in other teams' repos that changed designs; interview loops you improved.
**Not convincing:** "I'm always available to help."

## 5. Operational excellence

| Senior | Staff | Principal |
|---|---|---|
| Reliable on call for the team's systems; writes good postmortems. | Leads major incidents across teams; removes whole classes of incidents; sets reliability practices others use. | Sets reliability strategy and the trade-off between speed and safety for the org. |

**Convincing:** an incident class that stopped happening after your change (with the before/after count); the runbook or alert everyone uses now; the incident you led and what changed afterwards.
**Not convincing:** "I fixed many bugs."

## 6. Communication and alignment

| Senior | Staff | Principal |
|---|---|---|
| Explains their work clearly to the team and to product. | Aligns teams with conflicting goals; writes for directors and execs; disagrees in a way that moves things forward. | Represents engineering to the company's leadership and outside it. |

**Convincing:** a disagreement you resolved and what was decided; a doc that got leadership to fund or stop something; a long, contentious PR or RFC thread that ended in agreement.
**Not convincing:** "good communicator".

## What usually decides it

- **Staff is about scope beyond your team.** Committees look for impact that wouldn't have happened without you, across team boundaries.
- **Show judgment, not just output.** The thing you said no to, the risk you caught, the trade-off you made and why.
- **Others should be in the story.** Who you brought along, unblocked or grew.
